“To make the best of what is in our power, and take the rest as it occurs”

EpictetusDiscourses, Book 4

Interpretation

What this Epictetus quote asks you to notice

The operative phrase is “is in our power and take the rest as.” Read this as a claim about response under pressure, not as a promise that hardship is harmless or fully controllable. Ask which part concerns action, which part concerns judgment, and what the line might overlook in another person’s circumstances.

This page does not treat one sentence as a complete account of Epictetus’s philosophy. It uses the quote as a reading prompt: identify the claim, test its limits, and follow the source before applying it as advice.

About Epictetus

Epictetus (c. 50-135 CE) was a Greek Stoic philosopher whose teachings have profoundly influenced philosophical and religious thought through the centuries. Born into slavery in Hierapolis, Phrygia (modern-day Pamukkale, Turkey), his life story represents one of the most remarkable journeys in philosophical history - from enslaved person to one of the most influential philosophers of all time. A three-step reflection

  1. Name the event without adding an interpretation.
  2. Separate the response you can choose from conditions you cannot command.
  3. Choose one proportionate action that preserves both courage and care.
