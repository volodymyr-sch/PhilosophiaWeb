## Privacy and claims guardrail

There is a material public inconsistency:

- Apple declares **Data Not Collected**.
- The linked privacy policy says the application may collect IP address, pages visited, time spent, operating system, and other automatically collected information.
- Google Play says app info/performance and device or other IDs may be collected.

Until the public disclosures are reconciled, marketing copy must not claim “no tracking,” “no data collected,” or equivalent privacy guarantees. The site links to the live privacy policy without summarizing it. Other claims used on the website—5,000+ quotes, 50+ schools, 200+ biographies, offline access, widgets, collections, customizable notifications, and AI Philosopher—are supported by public store copy or version history.

### Product positioning gap

Many competitors own only one job: a daily quote, a Stoic journal, a widget, or beginner lessons. Philosophia can credibly combine four jobs:

1. **Daily ritual:** curated quotes, notifications, Home and Lock Screen widgets.
2. **Source-aware discovery:** author plus source work, supported by a positive review.
3. **Depth after inspiration:** 50+ schools and 200+ philosopher biographies.
4. **Personal knowledge library:** bookmarks, likes, rankings, custom collections, offline access, and AI Philosopher.

The recommended promise is therefore not generic motivation. It is **a beautiful daily philosophy habit that can grow into a personal library of wisdom**.

### Placement rules

- Assign one primary query to one indexable page to avoid cannibalization.
- Put the primary phrase naturally in the title, H1, opening paragraph, one descriptive subheading, canonical URL, and one relevant internal link—not in every heading.
- Use supporting phrases only where they clarify the answer. Do not add a meta keywords tag; major search engines do not use it.
- Give each guide an immediate answer, practical steps, a relevant product workflow, primary-source links, and a next action.
- Use the widget and beginner-app pages as the strongest install bridges. Use philosopher pages to introduce high-intent readers to filters and collections.

## Messaging hierarchy

1. **Hero:** Daily philosophy quotes, one thought at a time.
2. **Functional proof:** Read, save, collect, explore, and see wisdom without opening the app.
3. **Depth proof:** 5,000+ quotes, 50+ schools, 200+ thinkers.
4. **Trust:** Source-work attribution, public review excerpt, free download, current widgets and collections.
5. **Conversion:** Keep one equal App Store and Google Play pair in the homepage hero so the primary action is unmistakable. On interior pages, present the stores with equal visual weight in navigation, general calls to action, mobile download bars, and download footers. A platform-specific guide may use a single-store action only when the workflow is genuinely exclusive to that platform.

## Website information architecture

- `/` is a conversion-focused, one-screen landing page. It uses a muted inline product demo, one central pair of equal App Store and Google Play download buttons, and three factual library proof points. Its header and utility footer intentionally omit duplicate store links.
- `/guides/` is the crawlable hub for all eight long-form keyword guides.
- `/faq.html` contains the eight search-led questions and links each answer to the corresponding guide.
- Interior-page navigation, general guide calls to action, mobile download bars, and download footers present iOS and Android as peers. The iPhone widget guide keeps its article-level and sticky action iOS-only because it teaches an iOS-specific widget workflow; its global navigation and footer still offer both stores.
- The no-scroll layout applies only to the homepage. Guides and FAQ remain conventional server-readable pages so long-form answers stay usable and indexable.
- Essential homepage playback respects reduced-motion and data-saver preferences; the matching dark or light still poster remains visible whenever autoplay is paused or unavailable.

Avoid unsupported superlatives such as “the #1 philosophy app.” Avoid positioning the product as treatment for anxiety, depression, or other health conditions. Philosophy can be described as a reflection practice, not medical care.

## Post-launch validation plan

Public research identifies plausible demand; it cannot prove keyword-level installs or conversion rates. Validate the launch with first-party data:

1. Verify the domain in Google Search Console and submit `/sitemap.xml`.
2. Review impressions, average position, clicks, and click-through rate by guide and query after pages have been indexed.
3. Compare homepage, guide, quote, philosopher, school, topic, and work CTA clicks using the GA4 `store_click` event and the existing `data-location` attributes. The production measurement ID is `G-JCXBSEW87K`; custom event parameters are `store_platform`, `cta_location`, and `page_type`.
4. Add Apple campaign tokens and Google Play install-referrer parameters only after valid account identifiers are available.
5. Compare store product-page views and downloads with website CTA activity in App Store Connect and Google Play Console.
6. Refresh weak title/description pairs only after enough impressions exist; do not judge a new guide from a few days of data.
7. Expand the content hub from real query data—such as specific philosophers or schools—rather than publishing many near-duplicate pages.

GA4 is loaded only after analytics consent. Consent Mode v2 defaults analytics and all advertising storage to denied; granting analytics enables analytics storage while advertising storage, advertising user data, Google Signals, and ad-personalization signals remain disabled. Visitors can reopen the choice through the `Analytics settings` control beside the privacy-policy link.

## Source Lens library implementation

Implemented July 23, 2026 from the public English backend catalog:

- Quote endpoint: `https://mob.vlsch-serenity.org/quote?language=en`
- Philosopher endpoint: `https://mob.vlsch-serenity.org/philosophers?language=en`
- Philosophy-school endpoint: `https://mob.vlsch-serenity.org/philosophies?language=en`
- Source catalog: 5,618 English quote records, 25 philosopher profiles, and 18 philosophy-school profiles.
- Local quote snapshot: only the 150 selected records used by the public website.
- Initial publication: 150 individual quote pages, selected as ten named-source records for each of 15 philosophers.
- Editorial hubs: 15 philosopher hubs, 5 work hubs, 6 school hubs, and 4 topic hubs (30 total).
- Collection indexes: `/quotes/`, `/philosophers/`, `/works/`, `/schools/`, and `/topics/`.
- Publication data: `website/assets/data/quote-library.json`.
- Private build snapshots: `data/quotes-en.json`, `data/philosophers-en.json`, and `data/philosophies-en.json`.

Each quote page contains the exact backend text, author, source label, record identifier, a theme-specific reading lens, reflection prompts, app workflow, related quote links, canonical metadata, and Article/Breadcrumb structured data. Pages explicitly state that the backend does not provide an edition or translator and that the wording should be verified in a complete edition before academic use or republication.

Records whose source is only “Attributed…”, “Various Sources”, or another generic attribution are excluded from the ranked publication queue. This reduces the risk of presenting popular paraphrases as verified primary-text quotations.

There is no scheduled publishing automation. Only the 150 selected quote records are retained locally, and the public website remains fixed at those pages until a manual content update is requested. The public API key is not stored in the project.
